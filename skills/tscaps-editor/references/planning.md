# Planning an edit

Read this before writing a plan in your task list. Plan whenever a request needs more than two or three changes, and always for an edit of the video as a whole. A plan is how nothing gets forgotten: the work is not finished while a step is neither done nor dropped.

## Before the plan

Know the video first: read the transcript, look at it with `render_frames` (the start, the middle, the end), and ask the person what only they can decide. Then plan. When their answer changes the plan, rewrite it.

## What a plan holds

- **Every step the request needs, in the order you will do them.** A step is one thing the viewer will notice, or one check: "Reframe every shot to 9:16", "Hook: a title over the opening", "Key moment at [92 s]: the word set apart", not "improve the video".
- **What each step needs, in its own words:** the moment or the shots it is about, the guide to read first, the tool. "Reframe to 9:16: reframe_video." "Hook: hooks guide, split_shots before the first word after it, add_graphic."
- **Large steps broken down** where one can be half done: several key moments are one step each, so none is skipped.
- **A last step that looks at the whole result** before answering.

## Order for an edit of the whole video

The `direction` guide gives it (its section 7), and what the plan opens with: the video's card (its tone and the style that follows from it) and its beats, one line each. Every step after is one of those beats, or one line of the card being put in place.

## Working through it

- Mark a step `in_progress` when you start it, and `done` once it is made **and looked at**.
- Name each finished change with `title_checkpoint`, so the person finds it in the editor's history.
- A step that no longer applies is `dropped`, with the reason ("one voice only: no speaker styles").
- When you learn something that changes the plan (the person answers, a look shows a problem), rewrite it: add the step, reorder, keep the done ones done.
- Answer only when every step is done or dropped. The answer says what each step did.
