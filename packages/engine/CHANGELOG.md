# Changelog

All notable changes to `@tscaps/engine` are documented here. This file
follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the
package uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

Prior 0.1.x releases shipped without a tracked changelog; consult the
git history for their contents.

## [Unreleased]

## [0.8.0] - 2026-11-21

### Added
- `MediaBunnyRenderers` builds the engine's renderers ready to use: `videoRenderer` writes a `RenderJob` to a video file, and `stillFrameRenderer` paints any of its frames through the same compositing. A render with footage, audio layers, a grade or cuts no longer needs some thirty classes wired by hand. Its options take a `CssResourceEmbedder` and a `WordSplitter`; `overrides` (`MediaBunnyRendererOverrides`) replace or watch its decoders, encoder and sprite-sheet probes, and caption or overlay renderer; `parts` (`MediaBunnyRendererParts`) hands out what it built for a host to share, such as the transcode coordinator and the footage grader.
- `RenderPipelineBuilder` sets every field of the render job: `withFrame`, `withBackground`, `withFootage`, `withAudioMix`, `withInputAudio`, `withSkipRanges`, `withSignal`, `withOnAudioMixDropped`, `withOnAudioMixInputFallback`, `withConfirmUndecodableAudioLayers`, `withOnVideoFrameDecoderSelected` and `withOnVideoEncoderSelected`. Its default line splitter measures lines against `frame` when one is set and `outputResolution` is not.
- Types the public API named but did not export can be imported: `DocumentProps`, `SectionProps`, `SegmentProps`, `LineProps`, `WordProps`, `DecorationProps`, the `*RenderContext` types, `WordPosition`, `SubtitleCue`, `SubtitleRow`, `SubtitleToken`, `TimeRange`, and the errors a transcription can end in, `WhisperModelLoadFailedError` and `WhisperInferenceFailedError`.
- `TagBoundarySegmentSplitter` gives the words a `TagCondition` holds for segments of their own, apart from the words around them. `TagRoutedSegmentSplitter` and `TagRoutedLineSplitter` send each segment through the splitter of the first `TagRoute` whose condition holds over its tags and its words', or through a fallback, so segments of different kinds in one document can be cut and laid out differently.
- Footage layers: other videos, still images and GIFs drawn on the video's picture over stretches of the output, styled with CSS the way captions are. `RenderJob.footage` (`RenderFootage`) takes the `FootageLayer`s, bottom to top, and a `FootageStyle`: a stylesheet and the frame size it is written against. A layer holds only what it shows and when (`id`, `source`, output stretch, where its source starts, how often a video starts over); its `id` is what the stylesheet addresses its box by (`[data-tscaps-el]`). The engine resolves the stylesheet on a stage of its own, a size container the frame's size (`CssClass.FOOTAGE_STAGE`) with one box per layer (`CssClass.FOOTAGE_LAYER`), every animation paused and frozen to the layer's clock (`CssVariable.LAYER_STARTS`, `LAYER_ENDS`, `LAYER_DURATION`: `--on-layer-starts`, `--on-layer-ends`, `--layer-duration`). What draws is read off each box: its layout box, `transform`, `translate`, `rotate` and `scale` about its origin, `opacity`, a `blur()`, an `inset()` clip, its corner radius, `object-fit`, and `--video-left/top/width/height` (`CssVariable.VIDEO_*`), where the video's own picture is re-framed over black while the layer shows (the topmost layer's wins). `FootageLayerResolver` answers that for any moment, synchronously, and gives the same answer again for the same moment, so a live preview and anything else showing it can share one; `CssFootageLayerResolverFactory` opens one on a set of layers and a stylesheet, and `FootageStageBaselineCss` writes the stage's own rules for a consumer showing a stage of its own. `CanvasFootageCompositor` draws the resolved states, the clip, rounded corners and picture moving together, and a consumer's preview can draw through it too. A video covering the whole frame square-cornered and at rest hides everything beneath it, which is then not drawn (`FootageOcclusion`); faded, moved, blurred or cut, it hides nothing. `FootageLayerSchedule` answers which layers and which moment of each belong to an output time. `CssKeyframeNamespacer` and `CssKeyframesScanner` read a custom property whose name ends in `animation` as an `animation` shorthand held for later.
- GIFs play in browsers without a usable `ImageDecoder`. `ScriptGifOpener` decodes them in script (LZW, colour tables, transparency, interlacing, disposal), lazily and forward, holding the canvas rather than every frame. `FallbackAnimatedImageOpener` tries openers in order, so `ImageDecoderGifOpener` can stand in front of it. An opener that cannot play an animation rejects with `AnimatedImageOpenFailedError` instead of standing a still frame in for it. `ImageDecoderGifOpener` refuses where a frame decoded a second time cannot be drawn, as in Firefox, since every loop needs one. `GifFileReader` reads a GIF's block structure once; `GifFrameDurationReader` takes one.
- Footage layers whose pictures the caller draws: a `FootageSource` of kind `provided` (`ProvidedFootageSource`) holds a `FootagePictureProvider` that hands over a picture, transparency included, for each moment of the layer's stretch. The layer is styled, timed and composited like any other, so a host can draw something no file holds, such as a page it renders itself, and have it placed, moved and faded by the stylesheet.
- A colour grade over the composed picture: `RenderFootage.grade` (`FootageGrade`: exposure, contrast, saturation, temperature, tint, vignette). `CanvasFootageCompositor` takes a `FootageGrader` and grades last, beneath the captions; `WebGlFootageGrader` grades in a WebGL2 shader and hands over to `CanvasFilterFootageGrader` where WebGL2 is missing. `FootageGradeMath` is the one place the terms come from. `SourcePictureAsIs` never holds under a grade.
- `RenderJob.frame` sets the output's size before `outputResolution`, so the output can take another shape than the input; with no layer showing the source's picture, it covers the frame, centred. Source-picture boxes carry `CssClass.FOOTAGE_SOURCE_PICTURE`.
- Still frames: `MediaBunnyStillFrameRenderer` opens a `StillFrameSession` on a job (`StillFrameJob`, the part of a `RenderJob` that says what its frames look like) and paints any of its output frames as an `ImageBitmap`, in any order, through the same painter, compositing and canvas set-up a render uses, so a still is the frame the render writes at the same moment. A moment inside a skipped range shows the first frame after it. Its painter needs caption sources that answer moments in any order: `BrowserRandomAccessSubtitleLayerSourceFactory` builds them (`RandomAccessSubtitleLayerSource`), landing each moment on the caption tick a render in order lands on.
- `SourcePicturePlacement` says where a composition draws the source's own picture, and `PlacedSourcePicture` carries it: the state the picture is drawn in on the frame, and the frame's and the picture's sizes. Drawing something measured on the source's picture with a `FootageFraming` of that state puts it where the picture shows it, cropped, zoomed or re-framed. `CanvasFootageCompositor` draws by the same rule.
- A footage layer can show the video's own picture: `FootageSource` of kind `source-picture` (`FootageSourcePicture`), with no file. Its box is styled and timed like any layer's, so the video can be placed, cropped, moved, scaled or faded over a stretch with CSS. Where such a layer is active the video is drawn only by it, over black, and in a layer's `--video-*` rect instead of its own box where one re-frames the video. `SourcePictureAsIs` says when a composition would draw the video exactly as it is, so a consumer can skip composing it; the export does.
- `object-position` is read off a footage box (`FootageLayerFrameState.position`, `FootagePosition`): a picture covered or contained in its box sits where it says, as a share of the room left and a move.
- A footage layer can draw the source's picture over itself rather than beneath every layer: `--video-above: 1` on its box (`CssVariable.VIDEO_ABOVE`, read as `FootageLayerFrameState.videoAbove`) draws the picture re-framed into its `--video-*` rect right after that layer, with its corners rounded by `--video-radius` (`CssVariable.VIDEO_RADIUS`, `videoCornerRadius`), and the frame beneath every layer starts black. A whole-frame rect is kept as a rect there, so the picture can grow to and shrink from the whole frame.
- Backdrop footage layers: a `FootageSource` of kind `'backdrop'` has no file. The layer takes up the frame drawn beneath it, the source's picture and every layer below, and draws it again in its own state over black. A layer that fades, blurs or moves the whole frame, such as a transition between two stretches of the output, is a backdrop layer styled like any other. It hides nothing beneath it. `BackdropFootage` is what `MediaBunnyFootageOpener` opens for it, a placeholder whose picture draws nothing, for a consumer composing through `CanvasFootageCompositor` on its own. A source read from a file is a `FileFootageSource`.
- Footage layers draw the amount of a `brightness()` in `filter` (`FootageLayerFrameState.brightness`, 1 for none), alongside the blur.
- A backdrop layer is drawn over its box's `background-color` (`FootageLayerFrameState.backgroundColor`, null where transparent), black where it has none, so fading it shows that colour. `CanvasFootagePicture` draws a canvas as a footage picture; `FootageFramedOverlayFrame` draws an overlay in the states of the backdrops over it.
- Where backdrop layers are active, the top layer (the actor cut out over the captions) is cut from the frame composed beneath them and drawn in their states, so it moves, blurs and fades with the picture. `FootageFrameComposer.frameFor` now answers a `ComposedFootageFrame`: the frame shown and, where backdrops are active, the frame beneath them with their states.
- Audio layers: other sounds mixed into the output over stretches of output time. `RenderJob.audioMix` (`RenderAudioMix`) takes the `AudioLayer`s and the level of the input's own audio (`inputGain`). A layer plays its source from a point, once or starting over every `sourcePassSec`, at a gain shaped by a fade in, a fade out and an optional `AudioDuck` that lowers it over given ranges. `AudioGainEnvelope` turns that into the breakpoints of a piecewise-linear gain, which a Web Audio `GainNode` plays back exactly as the export applies it. Layers are placed to the sample and the input's audio is cut to the sample at skipped ranges, with a 5 ms fade at each cut edge. The mix is encoded in the first compressed codec the output container holds that the browser, or an encoder registered with mediabunny's `registerEncoder`, can encode (AAC, then Opus, in MP4); `EncodableAudioMixCodecSelector` answers the same question before a render. When none can be encoded, the input's audio is written as before and `onAudioMixDropped` says why. Where the browser says it decodes the input's audio and WebCodecs then fails on it, at the first packet or partway, the mix decodes it through the Web Audio API instead and goes on (`onAudioMixInputFallback`, with the WebCodecs error); where that fails too before anything is written, the mix is dropped the same way, with `undecodable-input-audio`. That Web Audio path is `WebAudioAudioTrackSampleDecoder` (an `AudioTrackSampleDecoder`), the segmented decode `WebAudioAudioDecoder` reads through, and `DefaultAudioTrackBridgeFactory` takes one. A layer whose source cannot be decoded rejects the render with `AudioLayerDecodeFailedError`, naming the layer. Without layers, an `inputGain` other than 1 still mixes when the input's audio is kept and there is some, so its level changes; with an `inputGain` of 1, or the input's audio discarded or absent, a render writes its audio exactly as before.
- `LayoutWidthReader`, how wide an element is laid out as a multiple of its font size, read the way the renderer reads the widths it publishes. For a host that publishes the same widths from its own DOM.
- `IsolatedWordLineSplitterConfig.tagPriority`, tag conditions tried before the letter count, in order: the first that finds a word on screen long enough lifts it, whatever its length. A segment where none finds a word falls back to the letters. `searchFrom: 'start'` lifts the qualifying word closest to the start of the segment instead of the end.
- `SegmentProbeMarkupBuilder`, the markup one word of a segment is painted in at a given instant, built by the same builder the render uses. For a host that mounts it hidden under its own stylesheet and reads `getComputedStyle` to learn how the style resolves for that word.
- A `MediaBunnyTranscodeRequest` without a `painter` encodes each frame as it was decoded, without drawing it on a canvas: the output keeps the source's size, and a rotated source keeps its rotation as the track's metadata instead of in its pixels. A smaller `outputResolution` is refused, since nothing scales the frames. Converting a 60 s portrait 1080p HEVC this way took about the same time in headless Chromium on Linux, with 17% less CPU, 61% less main-thread time and 45% less memory, and 24–39% less time in Chrome and Edge on Windows machines without a GPU.
- `IsolatedWordLineSplitterConfig` gains rules for which word a segment lifts and which segments lift one: `allowFirstWord`, `minSegmentWords`, `minLineChars`, `minPlainSegmentsBetween` (the segments that lift are then chosen together, keeping the spacing and favouring the words said most slowly), `pickBy` (`position` or `spoken-duration`, `IsolatedWordPick`), `onlyAtEnds`, and `maxEdgeSpreadEm`, which keeps a word whose letters end unevenly from having a line on that side. That last one reads words through a `WordEdgeReader` passed to the splitter; `DomProbeWordEdgeReader` measures them as the style draws them. The lifted line is tagged `isolated-line`, and its segment `opens-with-isolated-line` or `closes-with-isolated-line` when that line is its first or its last.
- `TagBonusCutScorer` rates a cut as another `CutScorer` does, plus a weight when the word before it satisfies a `TagCondition`, so a run breaks right after the words it should end a piece on.
- `GlyphInkReader` reads how far an element's text reaches above and below its baseline, ink rather than box, in multiples of its font size (`GlyphInkExtents`). Lines publish it as `--line-ink-ascent-em` and `--line-ink-descent-em` when the style reads either.
- `OptimalLimitByCharsSegmentSplitter` splits by character count choosing every cut of a segment together: of the splits whose chunks all fit `minChars`..`maxChars`, the one with the highest total, each cut adding its rating minus `cutCost`. A strong cut moves the cuts around it into place, and a cut the bounds do not need is added only when it rates above `cutCost`; with no ratings it gives the greedy split. A segment no split can bound goes to the `SegmentSplitter` it is given as a fallback. Cuts are rated by a `CutScorer`; `BoundaryScorePauseCutScorer` rates one by the word's `boundaryScore` plus an optional bonus for the silence after it (`PauseBonusConfig`).

### Fixed
- `WebGlFootageGrader` grades a canvas that holds another origin's pixels on its fallback instead of throwing, and sends every later grade of that canvas there.
- `DocumentEditor` keeps the explicit window a segment was given when one of its words is retyped or given an emoji. It used to drop it.
- `WhisperTranscriber` no longer skips speech in audio shorter than the model's 30 s window, which is all of a short recording and the last window of a long one. The part of a window with no audio is now filled the way the reference Whisper decoder fills it, with zero in the feature scale, instead of with the features of zero samples. Over that tail of digital silence a model could close a segment seconds past the speech it had written and go on from there: `whisper-base` dropped 11 of the 31 words of a 15 s recording, with no untranscribed region reported. A final window that ends by its own end-of-sequence with a segment left open is now decoded once more from where that segment starts, as the reference decoder's seek loop does; it used to be reported through `onUntranscribedRegion` and left untranscribed. Audio that fits in one window is audited like any other.
- `WebGlFootageGrader` lets go of a WebGL2 context the browser has lost: that grade goes to its fallback and the next makes a context again. A lost context used to leave every graded frame black.
- The widths published to a stylesheet (`--segment-width-em`, `--line-width-em`, `--previous-line-width-em`, `--word-width-em`) are the ones layout gave each box, before any transform. They were read through transforms, so a size derived from one came out wrong whenever the element or a parent was drawn at another size or angle at the caption's first frame: a line drawn smaller with `scale`, an entrance that starts scaled, a rotated caption.
- In Safari, and in every browser on iOS, a style that needs the video frame (`videoFrame.required`) paints it on every frame of a render. WebKit decoded a large frame slice, set as the layer's CSS background, only once something had painted it, so the frames of an export came out without the video behind the caption: in a five-second luna export in Safari 26.4, 103 of 120 frames. On WebKit, told apart by `navigator.vendor`, the layer now holds the slice as an `<img decoding="sync">`. Chromium and Firefox render as before. `BrowserSubtitleFrameRenderer.create` accepts a `videoFrameLayerMarkup` to choose otherwise. On Safari 18 and older, and on iOS 18 and older, frames still come out without the video.
- The first captions of a render are measured in the font the style declares. The measurements behind a caption's slice of video frame and its measured widths could run before that font had loaded, in a fallback face, so a blended word could show its raw colour at its ends and a word sized from its measured width could come out at the wrong size.
- A style that needs the video frame (`videoFrame.required`) and declares `rendering.padding` now paints the frame where it is. The padding displaced the frame layer (by the bottom padding, for a caption anchored by its bottom edge), so a blend or a filter over it showed the video shifted.
- In Firefox, a caption with a blurred `text-shadow` beside an element carrying a `filter` no longer renders with solid boxes behind its text in every frame past about 8192 px into a sprite sheet. `ImageDecodeSpriteSheetRasterProbe` now also draws that pair at the sheet's origin and its far corner, and refuses a size whose two copies differ, with the new refusal `distorted-edge` in `SpriteSheetRasterRefusal`. Firefox then batches fewer frames per sheet (10 at 720×1280); Chromium is unchanged.
- A render no longer fails on a machine whose hardware video encoder accepts the configuration and then rejects the first frame (`OperationError: Encoding error. (Unexpected frame format.)`, seen on some Windows machines). Before the output opens, one frame is encoded with the render's own configuration. If the browser's default encoder rejects it, the render moves to `hardwareAcceleration: 'prefer-software'`, but only once a frame has gone through that encoder too. Otherwise it keeps the default and fails as before. This costs one extra encoder setup per render, about 0.4 s at 1080p on a Windows machine with no GPU. `RenderJob.onVideoEncoderSelected` and `MediaBunnyTranscodeRequest.onVideoEncoderSelected` report the choice as a `VideoEncoderSelection`.

### Changed
- **Breaking.** The package has two entry points. `@tscaps/engine` holds what a render is described and run with: the document model, transcribers, splitters, taggers, effects, subtitle files, caption styles, the render job with its footage and audio layers, the renderers' contracts, `MediaBunnyRenderers`, `RenderPipelineBuilder`, and the errors a run can end in. It follows semantic versioning. `@tscaps/engine/advanced` holds the parts the renderers are built from, for a caller assembling or replacing one: the MediaBunny renderers and transcode coordinator, codec policies, frame decoders, encoders and audio bridges, caption rasterization and its sprite-sheet probes, the footage stage, resolvers, compositor and graders, GIF decoding, the stylesheet toolkit (`CssLayer`, `CssBlockSealer`, `CssKeyframeNamespacer`, the scanners), bidirectional layout beyond direction detection, baseline CSS, and the profiler. These change with the engine's internals, in any release. Every name kept its spelling; an import of one that moved now comes from `@tscaps/engine/advanced`.
- **Breaking.** `RenderPipelineBuilder.withSubtitleFrameRenderer` and `withOverlayFrameRenderer` are gone. Pass the renderers as `overrides` to `MediaBunnyRenderers` and its `videoRenderer` to `withVideoRenderer`.
- **Breaking.** `RenderPipelineConfig` carries the render job's fields as one `job` (`RenderPipelineJobSettings`, a `RenderJob` without its video, document and styles) instead of one field each.
- `RenderJob.video` takes a `Blob`, as `RenderPipelineBuilder.withInputVideo` does, so a fetched video needs no `File` around it. `MediaBunnyTranscodeRequest.source` and `VideoFrameDecoderRequest.source` take a `Blob` too, so a `VideoFrameDecoderFactory` of a caller's own receives one.
- `CaptionsOverlayFramePainterFactory` is now `RenderJobFramePainterFactory`, and `CaptionsOverlayFramePainter` `RenderJobFramePainter`: each paints everything the job draws on a frame, not captions alone. The factory takes, last, the `FootageLayerResolverFactory` that resolves footage layers.
- `TopLayerSource.frameAt` takes a third argument, `sourcePicture`: where the source's own picture landed on the frame it is handed, or null where a layer hides that picture entirely. The frame handed is the one composed, footage layers and grade included.
- `CanvasFootageCompositor` takes a `FootageGrader`. `FramePainter.begin` and `FootageFrameComposer.open` also take the source's display size.
- `FootageSource` is a union of `FileFootageSource` (`video`, `image`, `gif`, with a `blob`), `FootageSourcePicture`, `FootageBackdrop` and `ProvidedFootageSource`. `FootageCompositionRequest` gains `videoWidth` and `videoHeight`, the video picture's own size. `FootageLayerFrameState` gains `position`.
- `WebCodecsVideoFrameDecoder` rejects a read that reaches a frame the browser cannot decode with the new `VideoFrameDecodeFailedError`, which carries `decodedUntilSec`, the time up to which frames came out, and the browser's error as `cause`. Before, the browser's error (usually an `EncodingError`) arrived on its own, and a source damaged partway through could not be told apart from one the browser refuses.
- `--segment-index` numbers a segment among every segment of its kind, in document order across all the sections of that kind, instead of starting again at 0 in each section. A document whose sections of one kind are split by another kind's, as when the segments of two speakers alternate, gave its segments indexes that repeated, so a style varying an effect by the index repeated the same variation. `SegmentRenderContext.indexInSection` is renamed `segmentIndex` and takes that number.
- `VideoTrackEncoder` has `encodeAsDecoded`, which takes a decoded `VideoSample` as it is, and `VideoTrackEncoderFactoryRequest` takes the `rotation` the track declares. `MediaBunnyCanvasVideoTrackEncoder` feeds a `VideoSampleSource` and snapshots its canvas itself, where it used a `CanvasSource`. A `DecodedVideoFrame` may lend its `sample`; `WebCodecsVideoFrameDecoder` does. `VideoEncoderAccelerationRequest` takes the kind of `frames` the run hands the encoder, and the probing policy encodes its probe frame the same way.
- `MediaBunnyTranscodeCoordinatorConfig` requires a `videoEncoderAccelerationPolicy`. Pass `new ProbingVideoEncoderAccelerationPolicy()` for the behaviour above. Its optional `VideoEncoderProbeObserver` is told whenever the default encoder rejects its probe frame. The default `RenderPipelineBuilder` wires the probing policy itself.
- `DefaultAudioTrackBridgeFactory` takes an `AudioLayerDecoder` (`FallbackAudioLayerDecoder` over `MediaBunnyAudioLayerDecoder` and `WebAudioAudioLayerDecoder` decodes everything the browser can) and an `AudioMixCodecSelector`. `AudioTrackBridgeRequest` gains `outputDurationSec`, `mix` and `onAudioMixDropped`; `MediaBunnyTranscodeRequest` gains `audioMix` and `onAudioMixDropped`.
- `BoundaryScoreLimitByCharsSegmentSplitter` is renamed `GreedyLimitByCharsSegmentSplitter` (and its config `GreedyLimitByCharsConfig`), and its constructor takes a `CutScorer` after the config. It cuts as before with `new BoundaryScorePauseCutScorer(null)`. This is a breaking change for consumers that construct it.

## [0.7.0] - 2026-10-14

### Added
- Caption layers. A segment or element override can carry `stacking` (`CaptionStacking.UNDER_TOP_LAYER` or `OVER_TOP_LAYER`) and `blendMode` (`CaptionBlendMode`, the blend modes CSS and the 2D canvas share under the same names). Whatever an element leaves out follows its segment, and whatever a segment leaves out is the base layer: under the top layer, plain. Each layer is rasterized apart and `LayeredFrameCompositor` draws it with its blend against everything already composited, the top layer included. A blend therefore holds whatever stacking contexts the stylesheet creates, which `mix-blend-mode` written on the element does not, and one word can sit behind a top layer such as an actor cutout while the rest of its caption stays in front. A word or glyph still in its line is painted by a copy of its segment's subtree in which only it is visible. A blended raster holds nothing of the video, so a blended element that does not move reuses one tile.
- `RenderJob.background` paints the captions on something other than the input's frames: `{ kind: 'color', color }` for a solid colour, or `{ kind: 'transparent' }` for an output with an alpha channel (WebM through VP9 or VP8; any other format is rejected). The input still sets the output's size, frame rate, duration and cuts. A top layer follows the background: it is cut out of the colour, or, on a transparent output, erases the captions under it instead of painting over them.
- `RenderJob.audio: 'discard'` leaves the output without audio.
- A render whose background is not the video no longer decodes it: each frame's time comes from the packets alone (`PacketTimingVideoFrameDecoder`). Measured on an 18 s 1080×1920 source in headless Chromium, a solid-colour render went from about 12 s to 9 s.
- `SegmentSubtreeDecomposer` and `SubtreeIsolationMarks` are exported, so a live preview can split a segment into the same subtrees and copies the export paints, and stamp the same classes on them.

### Changed
- `CaptionsOverlayFramePainterFactory` takes a `SubtitleLayerSourceFactory` and a `CaptionLayerDemand` in place of one `SubtitleLayerSource`. It opens one source per layer the document paints in, each with only the styles that paint there, so a run with no `stacking` or `blendMode` opens the base layer alone, as before. `BrowserSubtitleLayerSourceFactory` builds the default source per layer, and `BrowserSubtitleFrameRenderer.create` takes the layer it paints through `options.layer`.
- `CaptionsOverlayFramePainterFactory.create` takes the `FrameBackdrop` to paint on (`VideoFrameBackdrop` for the previous behaviour), and its top layer is no longer optional. `MediaBunnyTranscodeRequest` gains `alpha` and `audio`, `VideoTrackEncoderFactoryRequest` gains `alpha`, `AudioTrackBridgeRequest` gains `wanted`, and `VideoFrameDecoderRequest` gains `pixels`. `MediaBunnyTranscodeRequest` gains `sourcePixels`, `FrameBackdrop` has `showsFramePixels`, and `VideoFrameDecoderKind` gains `'packet-timing'`. `SubtitleLayerSourceFactory.create` takes `framesHaveAlpha`, `FrameBackdrop` has `sampledByCaptions`, and `FrameCompositionRequest` has `blendCaptions`. On a background that is not the video, captions blend with nothing: blended layers are composited plainly, and a caption that samples the video gets an empty frame, as PNG with its alpha instead of JPEG, so it stays transparent rather than black or tinted by the background.
- `FrameCompositionRequest.captions` is a list of `CaptionLayerFrame` in paint order instead of one `SubtitleFrame`.
- `ComposedSubtitleLayerSource` takes a `SubtitleStyleFrameDemand` as its third argument, the one place that decides whether a style reads the video frame.
- `BaselineNeeds` gains `isolation`, the rules a copy of a subtree needs, so `composeOptional` callers have to say whether they want them.

### Fixed
- A caption that reads the video frame no longer erases the overlay beneath it. It was handed the bare frame and painted it back over the overlay wherever it drew, so a watermark disappeared inside a frosted caption's box. Caption sources are now handed the frame with the overlay drawn on it; the top layer still gets the bare frame.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-10-01

### Changed
- `RenderPipeline` splits lines after effects, so a line splitter reads each segment's time as it will be shown. The order is now segment splitting → semantic tagging → effects → line splitting → structural tagging → rendering. `runSplittingStep` is replaced by `runSegmentSplittingStep` and `runLineSplittingStep`, and the `splitting` progress stage by `splitting-segments` and `splitting-lines`.
- `IsolatedWordLineSplitter` measures a word's time on screen up to its segment's `time.end`, so an effect that holds a segment, or a time set by hand, counts. `IsolatedWordLineSplitterConfig.holdAfterLastWordSeconds` is removed.
- `PauseTagger` measures the silence between words, so a segment held on screen past its last word no longer hides the pause after it.

### Fixed
- `WhisperTranscriber` word times no longer run late. transformers.js times a token from the cross-attention row that reads it, which lands about a token late (0.2 to 0.3 s in English), and it aligns a short final window over its zero padding, so the last word could end after the audio. Each token now takes the midpoint of the row that reads it and the row that predicts it, and the frame count is halved to encoder frames. Measured against audio of known timing, starts land within ±50 ms on `base` and `small`.
- `WhisperTranscriber` no longer drops speech where two windows meet next to a pause. transformers.js merges the two windows' takes by matching tokens, accepting a match however much later the second take places it, so two unrelated sentences could be spliced on a shared "the" and lose every word between them. A match must now start within 1 s on either side. Both fixes patch private transformers.js methods on the loaded model and tokenizer, so a transformers.js upgrade has to be checked against them.

## [0.5.1] - 2026-09-27

### Added
- `IsolatedWordLineSplitterConfig.fallbackMinSecondsOnScreen`, a looser time on screen tried only when no word reaches `minSecondsOnScreen`. Absent, the splitter behaves as before.
- `LINE_NO_WRAP_CSS`, the rule that keeps a line on one row, exported so a consumer can emit it in its own framework layer and let a stylesheet override `white-space` on `.line`.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-09-27

### Added
- Model downloads are configurable as one `ModelAssetSources` value: the persistent file cache, the request strategy, and the URLs of the ONNX WebAssembly runtime. `DirectModelFileFetcher` names transport failures, while `RelayFallbackModelFileFetcher` retries an unreachable host through a relay without changing the cache key and remembers that host for the rest of the run. `WhisperTranscriber` now takes this value as its third constructor argument instead of a bare `ModelFileCache`.
- CSS resource embedding has an explicit outbound-request policy. `BrowserCssResourceEmbedder` now requires a `CssResourceUrlPolicy`; `AnyOriginCssResourceUrlPolicy` preserves the browser-rendering behaviour, and `SameMachineCssResourceUrlPolicy` limits a hosted renderer to `blob:` and same-origin URLs. The default `RenderPipelineBuilder` remains permissive.
- `IsolatedWordLineSplitter` puts one word of each segment alone on the second line, so a stylesheet can size it as that line. The word is the latest one with at least `minLetters` letters that stays on screen for `minSecondsOnScreen`, counted from when it is said until the next segment starts or `holdAfterLastWordSeconds` after the last word, whichever comes first. Words after it take a third line; when no word qualifies, the segment stays on one line.
- `--previous-line-width-em` on every line after the first: the measured width of the line above, in the same unit as `--line-width-em`, so a stylesheet can align two lines against each other.
- `FixedTailLineSplitterConfig.minWordsToSplit`, the fewest words a segment needs before it is split. Defaults to 3, the value the splitter always used.
- `VideoEncoderSelectionFailedError` identifies a render that cannot start because the browser supports none of the codecs accepted by the output format. Its stable `name` and `ERROR_NAME` survive worker and browser-automation boundaries.

### Changed
- An export decodes only the video it keeps. A cut of three seconds or more is seeked past rather than decoded and thrown away frame by frame, so decode cost follows the output's length instead of the source's. On a 60 s source with 54 s cut, 1439 decoded frames become 145: 3387 ms to 636 ms at 4K, and 1565 ms to 219 ms at 1080p. Shorter cuts are still read through, because a seek resumes at the key frame before it and measured h264 sources key every 1.0 s to 3.5 s. `VideoFrameDecoder` gains `canSeek()` and an optional span on `samples()`; a decoder answering `false` still receives the whole track.
- `SubtitleStyle.wordOverrides` is now `subtreeOverrides`. The broader name reflects that overrides can target lines and decorations as well as words. This is a breaking rename for consumers that construct subtitle styles.

### Fixed
- A line written entirely against the declared direction is laid out on its own base. `RenderingConfig.textDirection` states the language of the captions, so a caption holding one whole English sentence inside a right-to-left document painted as `[.] [This] [is] [a] [test]`: the full stop is neutral, its right-hand neighbour is the paragraph boundary, and UAX #9 resolves that tie toward the paragraph direction. `LineBaseDirectionResolver` overrides the base for a line carrying strong characters of one direction only, where the base moves the neutrals at the edges and can reorder nothing. A line mixing both keeps the declared base. `WordFragmenter` takes the resolver as its third constructor argument.
- A fragment of a word that paints away from the rest of it no longer carries the word's decorations. A word split across embedding levels is emitted as one element per piece, and a stylesheet drawing a background on `.word-being-narrated` painted it twice, at opposite ends of a mixed line: a right-to-left line closing on `this is a test.` paints as `[.] [this] [is] [a] [test]`, with the right-to-left words further right, so the pill landed on `test` and again on the full stop with three words between them. `WordFragment.carriesWordBody` is false on such a piece and the renderers add the `detached-word-fragment` class, which a rule that points at a word excludes. Pieces that paint flush against each other are all body, so a word whose punctuation lands beside it still highlights as one box. The state class and the timing variables stay on every piece: a stray is read at the same moment as the rest of its word. `WordFragmenter` takes a `BidiCharacterClassifier` as its fourth constructor argument.
- Segment and line inline styles land on the element they describe instead of being merged into an outer wrapper. Template rules can no longer override a chosen element font merely because the font was inherited, and line-level overrides now reach exported frames.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-08-30

### Added
- Stylesheets can read how wide an element renders: `--segment-width-em`, `--line-width-em`, `--word-width-em`. The engine mounts the subtree under the consumer's own stylesheet and reads the boxes back, so the widths account for the classes the element actually carries. They are multiples of the font size, which is what lets a rule derive the size from the width. Measured once per caption per style, and skipped for a stylesheet that reads none of them.
- `--segment-anchor-y` and `--segment-anchor-origin-y` report where placement put the caption and which part of the box landed there. A rule can position a caption absolutely instead of assuming an anchor the consumer may have moved.
- `--line-char-count`, beside the segment and word counts already published.
- Audio decoding without WebCodecs. `WebAudioAudioDecoder` decodes through `AudioContext.decodeAudioData`, `FallbackAudioDecoder` retries a failed primary decoder through a second one, and `MediaBunnyAudioOnlyRemuxer` copies a container's audio packets out so neither has to load the video bytes. Safari before 26 ships WebCodecs with no `AudioDecoder`.
- `WebAudioAudioDecoder` decodes in bounded segments rather than in one call, so peak memory is one segment plus the output whatever the source's length. `decodeAudioData` holds its input and its output at once, and a 38-minute recording died 5.3 s in on iOS 18.7. Measured on a 2290 s source in Chromium: 760 MB peak against 2884 MB, at the same wall clock. `AudioSegmentLayout` places the segments and gives each a margin of context at both ends, which the codec needs to reconstruct the frames at a seam.
- `Document.language` carries the ISO 639-1 two-letter code the document was transcribed in, `null` when unknown.
- `WebCodecsOnlyVideoFrameDecoderFactory` returns the WebCodecs decoder or throws `VideoFrameDecoderSelectionFailedError`. The default factory falls back to a hidden `<video>` element, which runs at playback speed. A host that would rather rewrite the source needs to see that refusal instead of a slow path taken in silence.
- `RenderJob` reports the decoder the run picked, through `VideoFrameDecoderKind` and `VideoFrameDecoderSelection`. Both are null when the run ended before a decoder was chosen.
- `MediaBunnyTranscodeRequest.signal` aborts a run. Checked between frames, so it stops cleanly, and rejects with an `AbortError`.
- `SpriteSheetProbeObserver` reports a host that can hold only one tile per sprite sheet. One tile per sheet means one rasterization per frame, and the capacity alone never said whether that was the host refusing or a legitimate answer. `SpriteSheetRasterProbe` backs the size walk and returns why a raster was refused rather than a boolean.
- `DocumentEditor.insertFirstSegment(doc, time, sectionKind)`, for a Document with no segments. `kind` is opaque to the engine, so the consumer names the Section.
- `Word.getTagCssClasses()` returns the classes a word's tags contribute, without asking for a timestamp.
- `BalancedLineSplitter` takes a `minCharsPerLine` floor. It uses one line fewer instead of leaving a short one.

### Changed
- **A batch covers as much video as the captions hold still for, not a fixed number of frames.** `getMaxBatchSize` is now `getMaxTilesPerBatch`: how many distinct pictures one sprite sheet can hold. `getFrames` returns a prefix of the timestamps it was given, ending where those pictures run out, so a caller offers a whole look-ahead and advances by the length of the answer. Passing at most `getMaxTilesPerBatch()` timestamps still comes back whole, so a caller on the old contract sees only the rename. The old shape assumed every timestamp was a distinct picture, so the sheet count was `frames / capacity` however well the frames deduplicated, and every sheet costs the same whatever it holds. Simulated over ten seconds of captions at 30 fps: 15 sheets become 1 at 21 tiles per sheet, and 300 become 20 at one.
- A caption bound to the video's own pixels batches too. Its picture only exists once the frame beneath it does, so it used to rasterize one sheet per output frame. `FramePainter.lookAhead()` tells the coordinator how many frames to gather, and `FramePainter` and `SubtitleLayerSource` now take runs of frames rather than single ones. At `lookAhead() === 1` the loop is the old one, with no copy and no retention. On a phone, a style doing this took 160 s and now takes 58.6 s.
- A style with SVG filters no longer rasterizes a tile per frame just for having them. Only a filter reading a value from the render context varies with time, typically a `feTurbulence` seed driven by a tick, and the cache treated every filter set that way. The planner now compares the filter markup each timestamp would paint, rendered through the same `SvgFilterDefsRenderer` the tile uses. A `var()` the scope leaves unresolved still forces separate frames: the document's CSS decides what it becomes, and the planner cannot see that. Measured on an export of 1702 frames at 720×1280 with an outline filter: 1636 tiles became 471.
- `SvgFilter` reports the custom property names its body reads (`variableNames`), and `SvgFilterDefinitions` the union across its filters. A caller can describe the scope a filter set is sensitive to without materializing it.
- A `SubtitleFrame`'s raster belongs to the batch that produced it, not to the renderer, and stays valid until `close`. Frames from different batches can be held and painted together, which a caller gathering a run across a batch boundary needs.
- `TextMeasurer.measure` takes the CSS classes the run will carry. It used to read one typography from a probe wearing no tags, so a rule sizing `.emphasis` differently was invisible and every word was measured as a plain one.
- The default `maxWidthRatio` is 0.72 rather than 0.8. The short-form players draw an action rail down the right edge, and the widest of them takes about a ninth of the width. 0.72 is also what 0.8 produced before the measurement fix below, so a template on the default looks as it did.
- `insertSegmentAt` handles only the anchored case and refuses a Document with no segments. Use `insertFirstSegment` for that one.
- `SpriteSheetSizeProber` is now `SpriteSheetSizeProbe`, and `BrowserSubtitleFrameRenderer.create` accepts one through `options.sizeProbe`. The size walk decodes rasters up to the whole pixel budget for an answer that depends only on the output size and the host, so renderers working at the same size should share it. Both were paying for it separately.

### Removed
- `SubtitleFrameRenderer.getFrame`. `getFrames` with one timestamp is the same call, and keeping both meant two raster lifetimes to document.
- `TextMeasurer.spaceWidth`. The renderer emits words as adjacent elements with nothing between them, so the gap is each word's margin and a space character was never part of a line.

### Fixed
- An animation on a pseudo-element no longer exports as a still image. The planner decided whether a caption was moving by mounting a chain of divs and reading `getComputedStyle` off the leaf, which never asks about pseudo-elements, so the tile was shared across the run and burned in while the preview played it. The question now goes through `AnimationStateFingerprint`, picked through `BrowserSubtitleFrameRenderer.create`'s `animationStateFingerprint` option. `keyframe-scan`, the default, reads the style's CSS once, including the inline styles per-element overrides set. `subtree-mount` mounts the subtree and asks `getAnimations({ subtree: true })`, which reaches pseudo-elements at any depth and resolves `calc()` delays.
- A line is measured at the width it renders at. The arithmetic was wrong three ways and came out 8% to 11.5% over the same words laid out by a browser: it counted a space between every pair of words, it spent letter spacing between a word's letters but not after the last, and it read one typography for every word. The corrections pull in opposite directions and the result now agrees with a real layout to within half a percent. Lines hold about a tenth more, so a template's width wants revisiting against what it was tuned to.
- `Document.withSegments` and `withMetadata` no longer drop `narrationPace`. Neither has a caller in this repo, so the loss was silent for anyone using the published package. Both clone paths now carry every field.
- `FallbackAudioDecoder` reports both failures rather than the primary's alone. Blaming the WebCodecs path on a runtime where it can never work is what told a reader to convert a video that was already MP4/H.264/AAC. The two travel as the `errors` of an `AggregateError`, since neither caused the other.
- `RenderProgress.totalFrames` carries a number. It was hard-coded to `0` at the only place the progress event is built, so a consumer showing "frame N of M" had nothing to put in M. It now comes from the output duration and the source's frame rate, both of which the coordinator already resolves, and the field documents that it is an estimate: a variable-frame-rate source has no single rate to read.
- A tagger keeps what the line it touches was already carrying. All five rebuilt their lines field by field, dropping anything tagging has no opinion about, and `WordlistTagger` and `RegexTagger` also handed back a fresh id, which is what per-line state is keyed by.

## [0.3.1] - 2026-08-15

### Added
- `onnxruntime-web` is declared as a peer dependency (`^1.27.0`). **Transcription does not work without it, and until now nothing said so until the model weights had already downloaded.**

  `@huggingface/transformers@4.2.0` pins `onnxruntime-web` to an exact `1.26.0-dev` build, and that build cannot open a quantized session against the current `_timestamped` Whisper exports. It fails with `TransposeDQWeightsForMatMulNBits ... Missing required scale`. The runtime side was fixed in ORT 1.27 ([microsoft/onnxruntime#28306](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/issues/28306), closed 2026-05-12); bundling that fix is tracked in [huggingface/transformers.js#1707](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js/issues/1707), where the maintainer places it in transformers.js v4.3.0.

  An exact pin cannot be lifted by a dependency range, so until 4.3.0 ships, a consumer that transcribes has to force the version at the root of their own tree:

  ```yaml
  # pnpm-workspace.yaml
  overrides:
    onnxruntime-web: 1.27.0
  ```

  ```jsonc
  // package.json, npm "overrides" or yarn "resolutions"
  "overrides": { "onnxruntime-web": "1.27.0" }
  ```

  The peer declaration only makes the mismatch visible while the tree is being installed. Resolving it is the consumer's override, and nothing this package can declare replaces it.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-08-14

### Added
- A stylesheet can address one rendered element. `SubtitleStyle.addressableElementIds` names the document elements that carry `data-tscaps-el` (`DataAttribute.ELEMENT_ID`) with their id, so a rule can target one word, line, segment or decoration rather than every element of that kind. Every painted fragment of a word carries it, including the pieces of a word the bidirectional algorithm splits across two embedding levels. Address nothing and nothing is stamped.
- A CSS toolkit for consumers assembling one stylesheet out of several sources: `CssLayer.FRAMEWORK` names the framework's own cascade layer, `CssBlockSealer` makes a hand-written excerpt safe to paste inside a wrapper, `CssKeyframeNamespacer` renames `@keyframes` per scope, `CssFragmentParser` splits a selectorless fragment into its top-level parts with offsets into the source, and `CssCustomPropertyDefinitionScanner` / `CssKeyframesScanner` / `CssSelectorClassScanner` / `CssFilterReferenceScanner` report what a stylesheet defines and reads. `BaselineCssComposer.composeOptional` covers a consumer rendering into a document that already supplies the universal half.
- `SvgFilterDefsRenderer` turns filter definitions into markup in one place: materialize against the render scope, resolve `em` / `cqh` to px, re-emit under the scoped id.
- `ModelFileCache` and `CacheStorageModelFileCache`. `WhisperTranscriber` now owns where model weights are stored instead of leaving it to the inference library, and a browser that refuses to keep them surfaces as `ModelFileCacheUnavailableError` rather than a console warning and a silent re-download every session.
- Subtitle-file writing, the inverse of the existing `SrtTranscriber` / `VttTranscriber` parsers. `SubtitleFileSerializer.serialize({ document, granularity, skipRanges })` returns the file's text, mirroring the shape `VideoRenderer.render` already takes. Six formats ship: `SrtSubtitleFileSerializer`, `VttSubtitleFileSerializer`, `AssSubtitleFileSerializer`, `SbvSubtitleFileSerializer`, `TtmlSubtitleFileSerializer` and `TextSubtitleFileSerializer`, each declaring its own media type and extension. Pass the same `skipRanges` handed to the renderer and the file lands on the same timeline as the video; omit them and nothing is rebased. `granularity: 'word'` asks for timing down to each word and every format honours it as far as it can, inline in WebVTT and TTML, with karaoke tags in ASS, and by emitting an entry per word in the formats that have no other way. Text is taken from `Word.text` rather than `Word.displayText`, so a styling pass cannot change what a file written from the same document contains.
- Bidirectional text support. Each line is resolved through the Unicode bidirectional algorithm and its words are emitted in painting order, so right-to-left and mixed-script captions render the way a browser renders the same text. A word spanning two embedding levels is emitted as one element per level. `RenderingConfig.textDirection` supplies the paragraph direction the resolution hangs from.
- `TextDirectionDetector` for inferring a text's reading direction, and `CursiveScriptDetector`, which letter-splitting consults so joining scripts are never broken into isolated glyphs.
- `ElementRenderOverrides.mergedWith`, layering a second set of per-element overrides over an existing one: inline styles merge key by key with the layer winning, alignment and classes replace when the layer sets them. Lets a caller derive extra per-element styling — a font that follows the writing system of each word, say — without rebuilding the overrides it already assembled.

### Changed
- A cue block that carries no timecode is skipped rather than refusing the whole file. `SrtTranscriber` and `VttTranscriber` announce each one verbatim through the new `onSkippedCueBlock` callback, so a caller can tell a file that was fully understood from one that was mostly understood, and only a file where nothing at all could be read throws — as `SubtitleFileUnreadableError`, which names the operation instead of the malformed byte. This is what the WebVTT parser algorithm prescribes and what a browser does with the same file.
- `VttTranscriber` reads the timestamps a WebVTT cue marks its words with, rather than stripping them and sharing the cue out by word length. A file that recorded when each word is spoken now yields those timings; one that did not is unchanged, and so is `SrtTranscriber`, whose format cannot carry them. Timecodes may now also leave out the hour (`MM:SS.mmm`), which WebVTT allows and the parser used to reject as malformed.
- `SegmentSubtreeHtmlBuilder` takes a `WordFragmenter`, and `RenderingConfig` requires `textDirection`.
- `AlignmentConfig.horizontalAlign` additionally accepts `start` / `end`, the way `text-align` accepts both vocabularies. A screen side leaves `horizontalOffset` counting from the left edge; a reading side mirrors the pair together, so one declaration follows the direction of the text. `HorizontalPlacementResolver` resolves the axis for a renderer, and `HorizontalSideResolver` maps a side on its own.
- Default `AudioDecoder` in the pipeline swapped from `BrowserAudioDecoder` (removed) to the new `MediaBunnyAudioDecoder`, which demuxes the source container with mediabunny and streams the primary audio track through `AudioSampleSink` instead of handing the whole container to `AudioContext.decodeAudioData`. Uses far less transient memory on long videos, covers containers Web Audio rejects, and reports `AudioDecoder.decode`'s new optional `onProgress` callback as samples arrive. A codec the browser cannot decode surfaces as a `DOMException` named `NotSupportedError`, following the WebCodecs convention.
- `WhisperTranscriber` runs on q8 quantization by default on WASM, reports real inference progress through `Transcriber.onProgress`, and adds `medium` to the model ladder alongside `tiny` / `base` / `small`. The inferring stage now carries a monotonic `[0, 1]` fraction of the audio processed so far instead of a single boundary event with no value; a `WhisperTextStreamer` wired into `model.generate` derives it from Whisper's per-window timestamp predictions. Consumers should pin `onnxruntime-web` to `>= 1.27` alongside `@huggingface/transformers@4.2` to unlock quantized session creation against the current `_timestamped` model exports (transformers.js#1707).
- Importing the package no longer pulls the inference runtime in with it. `@huggingface/transformers`, and the ONNX runtime it carries, are reached through a dynamic import the first time a transcription runs: an app that renders captions and never transcribes stops paying for them in download, and in Node the native binding is no longer loaded — on musl, no longer fails to load — for code that never runs. One consequence for bundling: a worker whose graph reaches the transcriber has to be emitted as an ES module rather than an IIFE, because a graph containing a dynamic import cannot be a single script. In Vite that is `worker.format: 'es'`.
- Document node ids are 22 characters instead of 36. `DocumentNodeId.generate()` replaces `crypto.randomUUID()` with sixteen random bytes in base64url — the same guarantee in fourteen fewer characters, which matters now that an id can be written into rendered markup once per addressed element per tile. Ids are opaque strings everywhere they are read, so documents holding the old form keep working and none is rewritten.

### Removed
- The `.section` styling contract. `--on-section-starts`, `--on-section-ends`, `--section-duration`, the `.section` element class and the `first-section-in-document` / `last-section-in-document` structure tags are gone; `Section` remains as the document grouping that decides which splitter and tagger chain a run of segments goes through. Nothing rendered them, so no stylesheet that worked before behaves differently — but a rule written against those names never did anything and now says so.

### Fixed
- A `<filter>`'s own attributes reach the browser. Both render paths rebuilt the element as a bare `<filter id="…">`, so every authored filter region was replaced by the SVG default (`-10%` to `120%`) and every `color-interpolation-filters` by `linearRGB`. Blurs and dilations that were being clipped at the default region now paint their full spread.
- The export's tile cache asks about an element rather than about its class. The probe deciding whether two timestamps share a rendered tile built its throw-away chain from CSS classes alone, so a rule addressing one element by `data-tscaps-el` never matched it and the tile was reused across a frame that moved — an animation played in a live preview and exported as a still image. It also never built a decoration node, so a glyph's animation was unreadable even coming from a stylesheet's own rules.
- `CssScoper` no longer swallows the rules after a statement-form at-rule. `@charset`, `@import`, `@namespace` and `@layer name;` are terminated by `;` rather than a block, and the scanner looked only for the `{` that opens one.
- A custom property a stylesheet only reads through a style query (`@container style(--name: value)`) is no longer stripped from the rendered element. It was dropped as unreferenced, so the rules behind the query never applied.
- `WhisperTranscriber` output no longer duplicates content across window boundaries or leaks the tail of a repetition loop. transformers.js chunk-and-stitch leaves both windows' takes on the same audio in the word-level chunks, and a decoder cornered into looping at the window boundary emits `<|t|>text<|t|>` pairs that the coverage record used to trust as legitimate segment closes. A new `WhisperChunkStitcher` splits the merged stream back into per-window groups (detected via timestamp drops in generation order) and crops each to one side of every overlap; `WhisperWindowCoverage` no longer advances its last-close mark on pairs where open equals close; and `WhisperLoopAbortLogitsProcessor` gates on `MIN_FULL_PERIODS` full repetitions plus `MIN_REPETITION_TOKENS` total tokens instead of a fixed detection window, uncapping the detectable period size while keeping short-phrase-repetition false positives at bay.
- `GapFreeEffect` tail padding is recomputed rather than accumulated, and now follows the words back down. It read `seg.time.end` — its own previous stamp — and added `maxGapMs` on top every time, so every keystroke fed through `reapplyEffects` grew every segment by another second. It also wrote that stamp to `Segment.customTime`, where it was indistinguishable from a window a caller had named, so the only way not to overwrite the caller was to refuse to shrink: a segment stayed stretched even once the following one had moved before its last word. Padding is now measured from the segment's own words and written to the new `Segment.effectTime`, which the effect owns and overwrites wholesale, clearing it when there is no room left to pad. `Segment.time` resolves `customTime ?? effectTime ?? wordTime`, so a caller's explicit window still outranks the pass, and a segment carrying one is skipped rather than padded over. `DocumentEditor.updateWordTime` also stopped discarding `customTime`: moving a word changes when the segment's words sound, not which words it holds.
