## How the synthesized files were made

Nothing in them is recorded or sampled: each is plain signal processing over seeded noise and oscillators, so
the same recipe always produces the same samples. Their authors dedicate them to the public domain under CC0
1.0.

- **whoosh** — pink noise through a band-pass filter whose centre rises to a peak and falls away (the pitch shift
  of something passing), a wide band an octave below it for the push of air, a fainter band of air above it and
  a low-passed sub swell. The level rises to its peak a quarter to two fifths in and decays exponentially after
  it, with a flutter that speeds up toward the peak, panned across the stereo field.
- **pop** — a sine gliding up in pitch, 420 Hz to 1150 Hz, and decaying in 22 ms, over a short low thump
  (110 Hz) for body and a band-passed noise snap on its front.
- **bell** — six partials at inharmonic ratios of 1318.5 Hz (E6), each with its own decay, one slightly
  detuned twin of the fundamental for shimmer, and a short strike transient.
- **riserHit** — the **riser** below for 1.5 s, cut into a **boom** and a **hit** laid together, so it lands on
  a hit 1.5 s in and rings out for about 1.3 s.
- **riser** — noise opening upward through a band-pass and a band-limited saw climbing two to two and a half
  octaves, swelling to full level at the end, with a tremolo that speeds up as it goes. `riser-short` and
  `riser-long` swell over 16 dB, play the saw as a pair detuned 0.6 % either way, one per side, and add a sub
  sine (45 to 75 Hz) pulsing with the tremolo and a high-passed noise that opens in the last third; the build
  of `riser-hit` swells over 30 dB without those layers.
- **boom** — a sub sine diving in pitch, a body tone, a low-passed noise crack on the attack and a mid-band
  noise tail, saturated so the low end grows harmonics.
- **hit** (part of **riserHit**) — a short thump dropping in pitch under a band-passed noise snap and a bright click, with a little
  room after it.
- **deskBell** — a desk bell struck twice, 140 ms apart, the second strike a little softer: five partials of
  2093 Hz (C7) at ratios 1, 1.0021 (a slow beat), 2.08, 2.92 and 4.4, each with its own decay, and a 3 ms
  high-passed noise strike.
- **glitch** — short seeded segments of square tones, bit-crushed noise, gaps and stutters, each panned on its
  own.

## Processing applied to every file

Each file, downloaded or synthesized, went through the same chain: resampled to 48 kHz stereo; high-passed at
20 Hz; silence below −60 dBFS trimmed off both ends; a 2 ms fade in and a short fade out; padded with under
20 ms of silence so the Opus stream ends on a whole frame; for a sound that plain gain would leave below
−20 LUFS (most clicks, the typing sounds, the punch and the pluck), a soft clipper (tanh at 4× oversampling) that
rounds off its highest peaks, driven by the least amount, up to 15 dB, that lets it reach −20 LUFS; one linear
gain that brings it to −16 LUFS integrated, lowered where that would put the true peak above −1.5 dBTP;
encoded as Ogg Opus at 96 kb/s. Every file's true peak is below −1 dBTP after encoding. Files cut from a longer
recording (`typing-short`, `typing-long`) start and end in the silence between two keystrokes, and files made of
several (`click-mouse`, `click-double`) lay them at the offsets above. `page-turn` is darkened before the chain:
high-passed at 250 Hz, then three low-passes in series whose cutoff glides from 6 kHz to 1 kHz across the file.
